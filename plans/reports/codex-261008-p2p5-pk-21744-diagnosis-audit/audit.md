## Required corrections

1. **The proposed readout contrast cannot identify the shortcut as the main blocker.** Report lines 124-128 interpret a successful taskhead run as proving that claim and a weak run as excluding it. Taskhead also changes feature dimensions, head initialization, gradient routes and optimization. Its query projections and higher-layer features can still depend on recent context; `r = S^T q` can carry information through a context-dependent q even when the bank is not episode-specific. Lines 63-64 should say that the direct final-hidden-state feature route is removed, rather than that every local route disappears. A success would support improvement from the changed readout recipe, with bank interventions needed separately to establish episode-specific bank dependence. A weak result would fail to show rescue by that recipe and would not rule out a shortcut interacting with other limitations. Define numerical thresholds, exact comparators and evaluation populations in a frozen proposal; “far above ... toward” is not an executable decision rule. A random-fixed taskhead arm answers an additional address-learning contrast; it does not remove these causal limitations. Keep it outside the authorized scope until separately selected and capped.

2. **L20 is weakly updating, not frozen or proved write-disabled.** Lines 92-96 contradict the exact tensors and full-precision telemetry. From step 50 to 1500, all 4096 beta weight elements and all four biases change. Weight delta L2 is 0.0199219249 (max 0.0005910993); bias delta L2 is 0.0006529555 (max 0.0003673583). The beta, k and value groups each have nonzero update norms on 133 of 150 logged rows. The four final binding gate means are 1.0550523e-5, 2.0088062e-5, 1.9314053e-4 and 2.4650402e-8; filler means are also nonzero. Rounded zeros and nearly unchanged norms do not establish exact zero gates, absence of writes, frozen parameters, or that closure caused the None gradients. Preserve the observed tiny gradients, small parameter drift, 17 absent-gradient samples, and low final gates. Their causal relationship and the onset of saturation remain unresolved.

3. **Bind the manually consumed evidence.** The owner's 55-input index covers its automated cells/readings analysis, not the eight checkpoints, two P2C metrics files and P2C RESULTS.md used for F2-F4. Add exact paths and hashes, reproducible tensor differences, P2C values and their sampling endpoints to the analysis output. Bind the imported paired-statistics source or explicitly reference its frozen commit. The reviewer evidence binds these extra files, but the owner report should be self-contained and reproducible too.

4. **Narrow universal saturation/selectivity language.** Lines 74-77 describe every per-head gate as near an endpoint. The saved data include intermediate means, for example learned-n32-s1 L6h1 is 0.3336249 for bindings and 0.3373898 for filler. Across the 512 saved head/class means, 66 lie strictly between 0.01 and 0.99 (that cutoff is only an illustrative descriptive count). Many head/class means are near an endpoint, and pooled binding/filler ratios are near one; neither establishes saturation or nonselectivity for every head or every event. Use the actual per-head exceptions and distinguish pooled means from event distributions. Cross-model, last-row-versus-phase-median gradient magnitudes remain descriptive.

Learned-n16-s1 has 23/1024 correct both with none and query_off, despite 918 changed predictions. Its none-minus-read-offset interval also contains zero. Describe prediction dependence broadly and accuracy differences per cell with intervals.
